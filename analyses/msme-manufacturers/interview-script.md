# Interview script: late payments (MSME suppliers)

**Goal:** find out whether late payment from buyers is a real, costly problem, and whether owners would use the 45-day rule to fix it. **Do not sell anything.** You are learning, not pitching.

**Who to contact:** owners or accounts heads of small factories that supply bigger companies (auto parts, CNC job work, moulding, fabrication, packaging). The contact list is `analyses/msme-leads/raw/google-maps/leads.csv` (local only).

**Time:** 5–8 minutes per call. Ask about **their last real late payment**, not about opinions.

**Proof target:** out of 50 contacts, about 15 conversations. The pain is real if **5 or more** say late payment is a top problem *and* have invoices over 45 days late right now.

---

## 1. Opening

**Call:**
> Hello, my name is [name]. I'm researching how small manufacturers deal with late payments from their buyers. I'm not selling anything. Could you spare 5 minutes? Your experience would really help.

**Message (only after an email or call, or if they don't pick up):**
> Hello [name], I'm [name]. I'm researching how small manufacturers handle late payments from buyers. Could we talk for 5 minutes? I'm not selling anything and won't ask for any payment, OTP or documents. I'd just like to understand your experience.

**Likely replies**

- *"Sure, go ahead."* → go to section 2.
- *"I'm busy right now."* → "No problem. What time works tomorrow?" Note the time.
- *"What are you selling?"* → "Nothing. We're only trying to understand how big this problem is."
- *"Talk to my accountant."* → good: ask for the accountant's number. They know the numbers better.

---

## 2. Warm-up: who they are

1. **What do you make, and who do you supply: big companies or traders?**
   - Likely: "We make auto parts and supply Tier-1 companies" / "We sell to traders."
   - **Note:** selling to big companies means the 45-day rule gives them leverage. Selling to traders is a weaker fit.

2. **Are you Udyam-registered? As micro, small or medium?**
   - Likely: "Yes, small" / "Not sure, my CA would know."
   - **Note:** only micro and small enterprises are protected by the 45-day rule and section 43B(h).

---

## 3. The pain (most important part)

3. **How many days does a buyer usually take to pay you?**
   - Likely: "60–90 days", "three months after the bill", "some take six months."
   - Follow up: **"And how many days does the agreement or purchase order say?"**

4. **Tell me about the last time a payment was very late. How much was it, and how many days late?**
   - Likely: "₹8 lakh has been stuck for four months", "One company took 120 days."
   - **Write down the amount and the days.** This is the strongest evidence you will get.

5. **Right now, roughly how much money is more than 45 days late?**
   - Likely: "₹20–30 lakh is out in the market", "I'd have to check in Tally."
   - **Note:** "I'd have to check" means they don't track how old their dues are, which is itself a product need.

6. **What does that cause for the business: loans, salaries, raw material?**
   - Likely: "We run on an overdraft and pay interest", "Salaries get delayed", "We buy raw material on credit."

7. **What do you do today to get paid?**
   - Likely: "We keep calling", "My accountant follows up", "We visit them", "Nothing, we just wait."
   - Follow up: **"How much time does that take each week?"**

---

## 4. The law: do they know it, and would they use it?

8. **Have you heard of the MSME 45-day rule? That buyers must pay micro and small suppliers within 45 days, or owe interest?**
   - Likely: "I've heard of it but never used it", "My CA mentioned it", "No, I didn't know."
   - If they know it: **"Have you ever used it? Why not?"**

9. **If a letter went to your buyer saying the payment is past 45 days, with the interest they owe and the tax deduction they lose, would you send it?**
   - Likely, positive: "Yes, if it's written politely", "Not to old buyers, but to new ones, yes."
   - Likely, negative: **"No, the buyer will get angry and stop ordering."**
   - **This answer decides the idea.** If most say no, the wedge must be softer: tracking and friendly reminders first, and legal letters only for buyers they've already lost.

10. **If software did this for you automatically (a 45-day timer on every invoice, reminders, the letter), would you pay ₹3,000–5,000 a month?**
    - Likely: "Yes, if money comes in faster", "Can I try it free first?", "Not that much; ₹1,000 maybe."
    - Follow up: **"If even one invoice came in 30 days sooner, what would that be worth to you?"**

---

## 5. Close: always ask for a next step

> Thank you, this was really helpful. Could I see 2–3 of your late invoices as an example? We'd prepare a free letter showing your buyer the interest they owe and the tax deduction they lose.

- **Yes** → the strongest proof. Note it as "next step agreed".
- Ask: **"Do you know other owners who face this?"** Referrals are the best leads.

---

## Do not

- Don't pitch or describe the product before question 9.
- Don't open with "Would you use…?" Ask what happened last time.
- Don't argue when they say no. Ask *why*.
- Don't promise legal results.
- Don't ask for invoices, documents or bank details on a first contact.

**Pain is real if,** out of about 15 conversations:
- 5 or more have invoices more than 45 days late right now;
- 5 or more call it a top-3 problem;
- 3 or more agree to a next step (share invoices or try a letter).

**Warning sign:** most say "I'd never send that to my buyer". Then the tracking and reminder side matters more than the legal side.
