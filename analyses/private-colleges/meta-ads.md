## Insights

**Mostly colleges advertising themselves:** 1,107 ads from 594 advertisers, the large majority online-MBA and admissions ads from universities (Andhra University Online, Amrita Online, Symbiosis, SRM Online). Real sellers *to* colleges are few:

| Category | Advertisers (approx.) | Examples | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| College / campus ERP | ~6 | Aethermind ("College ERP… admissions, records, attendance, fee management"), Sweedu, MCB HigherED (mcbapp: "outcome attainment, faculty workloads, backlogs"), AdPixa, NextZen ("ALL SOFTWARE JUST ₹4,000") | **Thin** on Meta (vendors sell through demos and field sales) |
| Admissions CRM / enquiry management | ~6 | Meritto ("inquiries, applications, evaluations, and fee payments into one platform"), MCB Admission CRM ("40-day free trial"), Educrafters ("might not be a lead generation problem… a conversion problem"), Ablepay | **Thin** |
| Admissions marketing agencies | ~3 | Marketing with Govind ("More Student Leads. Fewer Serious Applicants"), Novaflow | Thin |
| **NAAC / accreditation software or consulting** | **~1** | YanthraLabs (campus analytics); MCB mentions "outcome attainment" | **Empty on Meta** |

**The key signal: accreditation grades sell seats.** 90 ads from 35 institutions put their NAAC grade in the ad ("NAAC A++ Accredited"). Google's "People also ask" echoes this: "Are NAAC A++ colleges good?", "Which is better, NAAC A++ or NAAC A?". The grade is a marketing asset tied directly to admissions revenue, yet nobody advertises tools that help colleges earn or keep it.

**Can Meta reach colleges?** Not proven. Software vendors barely advertise there. Colleges are heavy Meta advertisers for students, so they know the channel, but buying is done through demos, conferences and field sales.

**Prices seen in ads:** website + student CRM bundle ₹6,999 (aimed at coaching/education consultants); "all software ₹4,000"; an ERP promo at ₹2,999. These are low-end offers, not what colleges pay.
