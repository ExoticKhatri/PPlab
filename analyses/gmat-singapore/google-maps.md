## Insights

**Coverage:** 62 places from 3 Singapore searches, collected in 12.5 minutes. **"GMAT prep in Singapore" returns only 4 places:** Prep Zone Academy (4.3★, 118 reviews), The Princeton Review Singapore (5★, 41), Manhattan Review (4.8★, 9) and a second Manhattan Review listing (3★, 2). "GMAT tutor" (40, hit the limit) mostly returns **general home-tuition agencies** (SmileTutor, Tutor City, FamilyTutor, Genius Mind) and colleges (PSB Academy, Kaplan City Campus), not GMAT specialists. "MBA admissions consultant" returns 26, mainly study-abroad agencies (Jack StudyAbroad, AUG, OverseasEducation.sg, AECC) and a few MBA consultants. 95% have websites; ratings average 4.73.

**Read:** dedicated in-person GMAT prep in Singapore is a **very thin market**, a handful of centres. Preparation happens online (TTP, GMAT Club, official GMAC) or through private tutors found by word of mouth.

**What customers complain about** (164 reviews at ≤2★; most are about general tuition or study-abroad agencies, and 17 mention GMAT/MBA): tutor quality 58, money/refunds 51, support and communication 45, admissions/consulting 18, no score improvement 19.

**The two GMAT/MBA-specific complaints are the most telling**
- **Prep Zone Academy (GMAT + admissions, ~S$7,000):** "I was promised a comprehensive package of admissions consulting services for a sum of nearly **$7000**… I diligently logged **42 hours of Quant Practice** (tutoring, classes, and self practice)… The tutors were hit or miss… This resulted in **no improvement — I got the same score from 5 years ago and from my diagnostic**."
- **Shantanu Sharma Consulting (MBA admissions):** "They chased me daily during sales… once I paid, the enthusiasm vanished… I was never told they rely on an **AI model** to turn my notes into essays. If I have to create pointers myself, I could just use ChatGPT… Paid for 2 schools; 1 partially done, 1 never started."

**Takeaway:** Singapore applicants pay **thousands of dollars** for bundled GMAT + admissions help, and the failures are the same ones seen in the US groups: no measurable score gain after many hours, tutors of uneven quality, and hidden use of AI. The local supply of dedicated GMAT prep is small enough that an online product doesn't compete with strong local incumbents.
