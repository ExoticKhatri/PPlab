**Data collected:** 858 Meta ads (194 advertisers, Singapore) · 12 apps / 5,930 Play reviews (Singapore store) · 59 videos / 2,584 YouTube comments · 1,040 Google autocomplete searches + 6 result pages (Singapore) · 8 Trends terms (Singapore) · 842 Reddit posts / 1,416 comments · 62 places / 528 Maps reviews (Singapore).

## 1. The group

Working professionals in Singapore preparing for the GMAT (Focus Edition) to apply to MBA programmes: INSEAD (Singapore campus), NUS, NTU Nanyang, SMU, and US/European schools. Many are expatriates, especially Indian and Chinese professionals. Prep happens around full-time jobs, usually over 2–6 months, often with several attempts. Interest is rising: **"mba" +44% YoY** in Singapore searches. Group size isn't measured here; Singapore is a small market (absolute search volumes are low), so this group is best seen as one high-value slice of the regional GMAT/MBA market.

## 2. Can we reach them? Yes, and the prep side is nearly uncontested

- **Meta:** business schools spend heavily (INSEAD Executive Education 88 ads, INSEAD 62, Nanyang 35, IE, IMD, NUS, HKUST, MBA fairs), but **GMAT-prep advertisers are almost absent**: GMAC (official, 58 ads), Magoosh (mostly GRE), INSEAD's own GMAT/GRE guide, and one verbal-specific advertiser (gmatverbal.niopractice.com). No TTP, e-GMAT, Manhattan Prep or Kaplan GMAT ads.
- **Search:** strong local intent ("gmat classes singapore price", "gmat focus score for insead / nus", "gmat test centre singapore").
- **Local supply:** only 4 GMAT-prep places on Maps (Prep Zone, The Princeton Review, Manhattan Review). Prep is online.
- **Community:** the global r/GMAT (94K) and GMAT Club. Singapore subreddits carry little GMAT talk.

## 4. Competitors and prices

- **Online platforms:** Target Test Prep (the rising default: "ttp gmat" Breakout, +400% around "gmat prep", 141 Reddit mentions), e-GMAT, Manhattan Prep, Magoosh (IAP $370–500), GMAT Club (free, community), GMAC official materials (the trusted standard).
- **Local Singapore:** Prep Zone Academy (GMAT + admissions bundles around S$7,000), The Princeton Review Singapore, Manhattan Review; plus general home-tuition agencies.
- **Admissions consultants and study-abroad agencies:** 26 on Maps; complaints about sales-heavy service.
- **Free:** GMAT Ninja and GMAT Club YouTube, "700+ with free material" videos.

## 5. Opportunities spotted in the data

1. **Plateau-breaker / mistake-pattern coach for GMAT Focus** (quant, DI and verbal). It would analyse each wrong answer, find the recurring reasoning trap, and drill it, built for people studying around full-time work. Evidence: pains #1, #2 and #5; tutoring and bundles cost thousands with uneven results; a near-empty Meta prep market in Singapore. The same engine generalises across LSAT, MCAT CARS and GMAT verbal (shared reasoning skills).
   - Risk: TTP dominance; practice must feel like the official exam (AI-made questions are rejected when they don't); Singapore alone is small, so it must sell regionally or globally.
2. **Verbal-specific GMAT product (CR + RC)**, as a niche within option 1. Evidence: verbal is the late-stage lever on Reddit, and there's one existing verbal-only advertiser in Singapore.
   - Risk: verbal is a small search niche; better as a module than a standalone product.
3. **Score-target and timeline planner for INSEAD/NUS applicants** (score needed, months available, plan around a job). Evidence: pain #3.
   - Risk: low willingness to pay on its own; works as a free lead magnet.

## 6. Data gaps

- **Reddit:** Singapore subreddits are mostly unrelated local content; GMAT signal comes from the global r/GMAT. The run paused on a rate limit and resumed successfully.
- **YouTube:** no country filter; global and Indian-creator content.
- **Google:** some non-Singapore autocomplete leaked in (Indian cities, London); one result page had no "People also ask" block.
- **Trends:** "gre" growth is AMD graphics-card noise; GMAT terms are too small in Singapore for % changes.
- **Not collected:** GMAC test-taker counts for Singapore, INSEAD/NUS class profiles, GMAT Club forum data, WhatsApp/Telegram study groups, LinkedIn.
