## Insights

**Coverage:** the run was stopped by the user after 125 of a planned ~180 offices. Mumbai, Delhi, Bengaluru and Ahmedabad are complete (30 each, every one "hit the limit", so more exist). Jaipur has only 5 and Indore wasn't reached. 1,423 reviews were read (12 lowest-rated per office), 1,080 of them with text.

**Group size and digital maturity**
- Dense: every city search maxed out at 30, and Maps lists up to ~120 per search, so these are a small sample of a large group. pplab's estimate is ~1 lakh CA/accounting firms in India (`group-data.ts`, unsourced).
- **Digitally mature for a small-business group:** 87% have a website, 98% list a phone, owners replied to 53% of the reviews read, and **no listing had reviews switched off** (vs 69% of schools).
- **Ratings are inflated and not useful:** average 4.90★, median 169 reviews per office. The low reviews mention review pressure: "I was uncomfortable with the pressure to give a 5-star rating and write a positive review" (R J Gala & Associates); "He use to pay 100rs to their employee for Google review". Google reviews are themselves a marketing channel CA firms actively work.
- Categories: Chartered accountant (91), Certified public accountant (29). Few chains: N D Savla (7 branches, 4 low reviews) is the largest.

**Takeaway:** what goes wrong between a CA firm and its clients is **communication and status visibility**: no updates, unanswered calls, unclear charges, work passed to juniors without handover. Fee disputes come second. This matches the vendor ad "A client calls: 'What's the status of my return?'…" (Meta) and the Reddit threads about opaque quotes. It points to a client-facing status/communication layer for CA firms, not more filing software.
