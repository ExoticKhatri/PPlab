## Insights

**Coverage:** 98 places across 6 cities (New York returned only 8; the others hit the 20 cap). 787 reviews were read, 709 with text. Collected in 17.5 minutes with 3 tabs. **In-person MCAT prep is a general tutoring market:** 51 "Tutoring service", 14 education centres, 8 consultants. Many places tutor SAT, GMAT, LSAT and MCAT together (Manhattan Elite, Princeton Review, TestMasters, Varsity Tutors, Wyzant). **Almost nothing is CARS-specific**: only 4 low reviews mention CARS, verbal or reading. 97% have websites; ratings average 4.81, so low reviews are a minority.

**What customers complain about** (167 reviews at ≤2★; keyword themes, overlapping)

| Theme | Reviews | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Money: refunds, price, cancellation, "not worth it" | 59 | 35% |
| Tutor quality / lack of structure | 44 | 26% |
| Support and communication | 33 | 20% |
| Scheduling / tutor availability | 23 | 14% |
| No score improvement | 22 | 13% |

Chains with the most low reviews: TestMasters (13, 8%) and Princeton Review (12, 7%).

Real quotes:

- "After investing the resources, I received **four points lower** on my actual exam than my starting practice exam 6 months earlier… The tutoring was just not what I expected." (Elite Medical Prep)
- "4-1 hour sessions at $329" (Varsity Tutors); "The tutor (there is only one available) seemed distracted… minimal feedback" (Next Level Education); "Scheduling regular, consistent sessions was incredibly difficult" (Elite Prep LA).

**Takeaway:** paying customers of human tutoring complain about **price vs result, tutor quality and scheduling**. These are the same complaints as the $3,499 Jack Westin story on Reddit. Local in-person prep doesn't specialise in CARS, so it isn't the competition for a CARS-focused product; online platforms and free YouTube are.
